# Gamification Research Notes

> Source: [I Studied 500+ Gamified Apps (Here's What Actually Works)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LXX_qOA5D8E)

---

## What Fails

**Points, Badges & Leaderboards (PBL)**
The "scoreboard" of an app — not the game itself. Drives quantity-over-quality behavior. LinkedIn and Foursquare both abandoned this model.

**Feature Overload**
Streaks + points + badges together create cognitive overload. Beyond a threshold, more features *reduce* engagement (S-curve problem).

**Long Streaks**
Eventually shift from motivational to obligational — cause FOMO, burnout, negative mental health. Increasingly under regulatory scrutiny.

---

## What Works

**Hyper-Local Competition**
Compete on small, specific segments — not global rankings. Strava's segment model makes winning *feel achievable*. Users don't compete against the world; they compete against their neighborhood.

**Variable Reward Magnitude**
Anticipation > certainty. Unpredictable rewards (like opening card packs) create curiosity and re-engagement. Predictable rewards lose their pull fast.

**Completion Drive**
Humans are hardwired to finish incomplete patterns (Gestalt closure). Apple Watch rings work because of this. Design UI around *closing loops*, not just earning points.

**Mastery Over Attendance**
Reward getting better, not just showing up. Peloton and Chess.com succeed by giving feedback that signals real skill development — not participation trophies.

---

## Takeaway

Gamification should enable mastery, leverage the urge to finish what's started, and foster healthy achievable competition — not trap users in the app at all costs.
